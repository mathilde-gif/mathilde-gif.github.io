About

I am a cognitive neuroscientist with clinical training in neuropsychology. Working with children taught me to pay attention to how they learn, adapt and make sense of the world. Those questions now guide my work on AI.

I co-founded everyone.AI and serve as its Chief Scientist. I am also Chief Scientist of iRAISE, the coalition co-led by everyone.AI and the Paris Peace Forum.

From working with children to working on AI

My PhD in cognitive neuroscience focused on how adolescents respond to cognitive training. In clinical practice, I used neuropsychological assessments to understand how children with neurodevelopmental differences learn and what support they need. That experience shapes how I evaluate AI for young people at different stages of development.

Later, while advising EdTech teams, I saw how often developmental knowledge reached a product decision too late. I co-founded everyone.AI to bring that knowledge into AI research and design early, and to let the questions people face in practice shape what we study.

The people who shape the work

I co-founded everyone.AI with Anne-Sophie Seret, Céline Malvoisin and Gregory Renard. Our backgrounds in research, technology, education and building organisations give us different ways to approach a shared mission for children.

Volunteers, researchers, engineers, educators and other experts help turn that mission into reports, tools and conversations. Young people’s perspectives and encounters across disciplines challenge our assumptions and shape the questions we take forward.

Away from the research

I recharge with my four children and through creative work, where curiosity gets to roam and making a mess is part of the process. When work feels demanding, my children bring me back to why I do it: the lives and futures this work is meant to serve. They also remind me how much children learn through trying, making and figuring things out.
