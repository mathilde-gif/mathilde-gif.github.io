Science in action

I believe evidence should inform action, and the questions that arise in practice should shape what researchers study. Through iRAISE and scientific cooperation, I bring developmental expertise into AI design, policy and adoption.

Building shared work through iRAISE

I co-founded iRAISE to bring developmental researchers, companies, governments, civil society and young people into the same conversation. Co-led by everyone.AI and the Paris Peace Forum, the coalition launched at the AI Action Summit in Paris in February 2025.

As Chief Scientist, I help frame shared research priorities and connect expertise across disciplines. The work spans product design, policy and adoption: practical questions inform what we study, and research gives collaborators a clearer basis for decisions.

Making developmental evidence usable

With Daniel Hipp, I led the scientific mapping of generative AI and child development in response to the French G7 Presidency’s call for contributions. It organizes anticipated benefits, risks and research priorities by developmental stage.

This gives policymakers and product teams a shared starting point: where evidence converges, what remains uncertain and which questions need further research. CALIBER develops the complementary measurement work, defining observable AI behaviors that teams can examine consistently.

Bringing evidence into international decisions

At the high-level dialogue during UNGA 2026, I contributed a developmental perspective on the evidence and cooperation needed to protect children as AI enters everyday life. This connects with my work through iRAISE on responsible deployment and adoption.

Research, institutional priorities and young people’s perspectives all have a place in that exchange. The aim is to make the questions concrete enough for people with different responsibilities to work on them together.

Advisory and scientific cooperation

I also contribute developmental expertise to guidance and scientific communities led by other institutions. These contributions have different scopes, from a defined advisory appointment to a panel or expert interview.

UNICEF: children’s rights and AI guidance

I am a member of UNICEF’s AI for Children expert advisory group, helping to shape the project and policy guidance, including Guidance on AI and Children 3.0. I contribute a developmental perspective on child-centred policies and systems.

IASEAI: connecting child development and AI safety

At IASEAI 2026, held at UNESCO in Paris, I chaired and joined the main-stage panel on AI and children’s rights. I brought a developmental perspective on the relationships AI invites and the questions this raises for evaluation, design and governance.
