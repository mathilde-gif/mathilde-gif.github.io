Research

I study how AI interactions may shape children’s learning, autonomy and relationships. These reports and methods bring developmental science into decisions about AI design and governance.

CALIBER

I lead the scientific development of CALIBER, turning developmental questions into observable AI behaviors and shared measures. Gregory Renard, our Technical Architect, works with the technical team to apply the framework through Aïa Safety Builder, an evaluation tool for repeatable assessments.

AI behavior needs to be described consistently before teams can compare responses or study their developmental effects.

Researchers, AI product teams and policymakers.

May 2026

Mapping of GenAI impacts on child development

In response to the French G7 Presidency’s call for scientific contributions on generative AI and minors, Daniel Hipp and I led a mapping of anticipated benefits and risks by developmental stage. It brings together international expertise to show where evidence converges, where uncertainty remains and which questions product teams and policymakers should address next.

Potential benefits and risks depend on age, use context and how an AI system is designed.

Policymakers, product teams and researchers setting priorities.

February 2026

Adolescents & Anthropomorphic AI: Rethinking Design for Wellbeing

Teenagers were using conversational AI for more than information, including reassurance and social interaction. Drawing on industry consultations, multidisciplinary experts and the iRAISE Lab, this report identifies the anthropomorphic, interactional and relational cues that need to be examined, alongside immediate guardrails and open research questions.

The style of an AI interaction deserves scrutiny alongside the information it provides.

Teams designing or governing conversational AI for teenagers.

October 2025

What AI Owes Children: A New Blueprint for User-Centered Beneficial Innovation

With Adrien Abécassis, I examined why developmental knowledge often reaches product and policy decisions too late. This chapter proposes a child-centered blueprint for beneficial innovation and explains how iRAISE connects research, companies, governments and civil society around children’s development and rights.

Developmental expertise needs to enter innovation early enough to influence its direction.

Policymakers, companies and partners building cross-sector work.

May 2024

The Future of Child Development in the AI Era

As AI began entering children’s everyday environments, developmental expertise and technical expertise were rarely considered together. This first report brought a review of the literature together with 15 experts across AI, product development, neuroscience and child development to identify the opportunities, risks and research questions that should guide decisions early.

AI changes children’s environments, making developmental knowledge essential to early design and policy decisions.

Anyone seeking a developmental starting point for children and AI.
