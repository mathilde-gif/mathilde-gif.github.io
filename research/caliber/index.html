CALIBER and Aïa

CALIBER gives researchers, product teams and policymakers a shared way to describe and measure how AI interacts with young people. Aïa turns that work into a tool, helping developmental science inform the AI children encounter.

The gap I kept encountering

The goal of my work at everyone.AI is to bring what we already know about child development into AI design and policy. But in conversations across sectors, I kept encountering the same difficulty: researchers, product teams and policymakers do not speak the same language.

A researcher may ask how an interaction affects a teenager’s autonomy. A policymaker may ask what age-appropriate protection requires. A product team needs to know which response to change. Connecting those questions takes more than sharing a report. It takes a common way to describe what the AI is doing.

Why we began with teens and emotional reliance

Adolescents are using AI for increasingly personal conversations, beyond help with homework. This is also a time of growing independence, changing relationships and learning to navigate emotions. We began with a question that connects all three sectors: how can AI offer support while limiting the risk of becoming a teenager’s main source of comfort?

That led us to examine how an AI creates a sense of connection and what kind of relationship it invites. A response can give sensible advice while suggesting that the AI has lived experience, will always be there, or understands a young person in a special way. Checking the advice alone leaves those cues unexplored.

The same advice can invite a different relationship

In my article Designing AI for Adolescents, I compare two responses to the same friendship problem. Both offer practical advice, but one adds claims of lived experience, reassurance and invitations to stay connected.

The second response claims a childhood and promises ongoing support. These are cues we can describe and measure. Whether they contribute to emotional reliance is a question for research with young people.

One framework, three ways to act

We created CALIBER to provide that common language. It starts with developmental questions and turns them into behaviors we can identify and measure in an AI response.

The same definitions, examples and measures can travel across research, product development and policy. That lets different teams work from the same evidence, while asking the questions relevant to their decisions.

From Social Sciences to a Socioaffective Framework

Presence Level Score

Trained human raters help us check that the descriptions can be used consistently. Developmental experts then help interpret which levels seem appropriate or concerning. In our first, small expert pilot, asking questions proactively was considered more acceptable in an educational conversation than when a teenager was expressing distress. A shared measure lets us make that distinction explicit and revise our guidance as evidence grows.

Bringing the framework into practice

Aïa Safety Builder applies CALIBER to AI responses. It checks the content of a response, then examines selected interaction behaviors and brings the findings into a report.

I lead CALIBER’s scientific development. Working with the technical team makes it possible to take those research definitions into repeatable assessments that others can use.

Gregory Renard, our Technical Architect, helps turn the research into the evaluation system behind the tool.

What we can learn now, and what comes next

The current beta focuses on English-language text responses for teenagers aged 13–18, looking at one exchange at a time in education, entertainment and emotional support. Our checks examine whether people and automated scoring can identify the defined behaviors consistently.

Measuring a response tells us what the AI does. Understanding what that means for learning, relationships or wellbeing requires studies with young people over time. Expert guidance provides provisional reference points for design and policy while that evidence develops.

We are expanding expert calibration across cultures and working toward assessment of longer conversations and other developmental questions. The aim is to keep improving both the measures and the decisions they support.

Building the connection together

Through everyone.AI and iRAISE, I bring researchers, companies and policymakers into this work. CALIBER and Aïa give those conversations something concrete to work with: the same behavior, the same measure, and a clearer view of which questions still need evidence.
